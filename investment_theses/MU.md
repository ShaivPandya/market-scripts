# MU
## Thesis
- Micron is the sole U.S.-based advanced memory manufacturer, operating in a structurally consolidated three-player DRAM oligopoly (Micron ~22%, SK Hynix ~39%, Samsung ~33%) with near-impenetrable barriers to entry
- AI-driven data center buildout is generating structurally elevated, supply-constrained demand for HBM and NAND; management confirmed supply growth will trail industry demand growth through at least calendar 2025
- TTM revenue recovered 141% from the August 2023 trough to ~$37B by August 2025, with gross margins at ~42% and an upward guidance revision of 4.7% in August 2025 (Q4 FY25 revenue guide: $11.2B ±$0.1B, non-GAAP EPS: $2.85 ±$0.07)
- Conservative balance sheet for a capex-heavy cyclical: Net Debt/EBIT of 0.62x, Debt/Tangible Equity of 0.32x, with debt maturities laddered long to avoid trough refinancing risk
- Valuation remains compelling relative to through-cycle normalized earnings power of $10–12/share; target price range of $230–$300 implies 75–130% upside from $131.37
- CEO Sanjay Mehrotra has demonstrated disciplined capital allocation — first to cut output in the 2022–23 downturn, divested non-core assets (Lehi fab, 3D XPoint), and maintained price discipline through margin compression

## Key Catalysts
- **HBM ramp:** HBM3 fully sold out; HBM4 entering production — multi-year supply shortage drives premium ASPs and margin expansion on the highest-value DRAM segment
- **Beat-and-raise earnings cadence:** September 23, 2025 earnings call likely to show further upside; management has established a pattern of conservative guidance followed by beats
- **AI capex cycle durability:** Hyperscalers treating AI infrastructure spend as existential, not discretionary — sustains both DRAM and NAND data center demand
- **Market share gains:** At ~22% share vs. larger peers, Micron has the most room to grow revenue in absolute terms as total addressable memory market expands
- **Geopolitical positioning:** Lower China revenue exposure (~12–16%) than SK Hynix (>20%) reduces regulatory and trade-war risk; existing China ban largely neutralizes incremental downside
- **Favorable macro:** Rate cuts reduce discount rates on capital-intensive earnings; full capex expensing lowers effective tax burden and improves after-tax FCF
- **CHIPS Act tailwinds:** As the only U.S. memory manufacturer, Micron is a primary beneficiary of domestic semiconductor subsidies supporting fab expansion

## Risk Factors
- **Cyclicality:** Memory is acutely cyclical — prior downturn (FY22–FY23) erased ~50% of TTM revenue; a demand correction could compress margins sharply and rapidly
- **Customer concentration:** ~50% of revenue derived from approximately 10 customers; loss of or reduced orders from any major hyperscaler/OEM creates material revenue risk
- **AI spending deceleration:** Any pullback or digestion pause in hyperscaler capex would reduce HBM and data center NAND demand faster than supply can be adjusted
- **NAND oversupply risk:** NAND market has more competitors and is more prone to oversupply; weaker NAND pricing could drag blended margins meaningfully
- **Technology execution risk:** EUV DRAM transition delays (management was transparent about prior delays) could widen the performance gap vs. SK Hynix HBM
- **China escalation:** Although existing restrictions limit incremental downside, a broader export control regime targeting all memory could disrupt supply chains or customer access
- **Valuation sensitivity:** Target price of $230–$300 assumes 23–25x on elevated through-cycle EPS of $10–12; a multiple compression or earnings miss compresses upside significantly
