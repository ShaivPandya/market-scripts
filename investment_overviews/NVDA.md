## Sensitivity to Extrinsic Factors

| Factor | Sensitivity | Capacity to Deal |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Commodity prices | Medium-high | Fabless model with multi-supplier strategy; directly procures some critical materials (memory, substrates); but limited supplier base and geographic concentration constrain leverage |
| Interest rates | Low | Debt is fixed-rate; investment portfolio sensitivity ~$0.2B per 0.5% move; large liquidity buffer of $62.6B |
| Currency/FX | Low-medium | Substantially all revenue denominated in USD; forward contracts in place to hedge residual exposure |
| Tariffs/trade policy | High | Export controls already foreclosed China data-center compute market at FY2026 year-end; $4.5B H20 inventory/obligation charge in Q1 FY2026; retaliatory actions and sanctions create ongoing disruption risk; policy shocks partly outside management control |
| War/geopolitical disruption | High | Supply and revenue concentrated in Taiwan (TSMC manufacturing), China/HK (9.1% of revenue), Israel, and Korea; geographic concentration flagged explicitly in risk disclosures |
| Regulatory changes | Medium-high | Faces regulatory scrutiny tied to AI market dominance; Arm acquisition terminated due to regulatory challenges; Run:ai acquisition required EC approval; export-control regime continues to evolve |
| Inflation | Medium | Higher wafer, component, and shipping costs flow through cost of revenue; partially offset by pricing power and long-term supply agreements |
| Labor costs | Low-medium | Not a primary cost driver given fabless model; R&D headcount is significant but not flagged as a material sensitivity in source disclosures |

## Industry

### Porter's Five Forces

- **Threat of New Entrants — Low**: Advanced-node foundry access, HBM supply, advanced packaging, CUDA ecosystem depth, software stack breadth, and customer qualification all require massive capital and multi-year execution; no credible new merchant GPU entrant identified in sources
- **Bargaining Power of Suppliers — High**: Nvidia depends on a limited number of foundries (TSMC, Samsung), memory suppliers (SK Hynix, Micron), and packaging/assembly partners; geographic concentration in Taiwan amplifies single-point-of-failure risk; HBM supply is constrained industry-wide
- **Bargaining Power of Buyers — Medium-high**: Top two direct customers represented 22% and 14% of FY2026 revenue; all are large hyperscalers with scale, capital, and growing in-house silicon programs (custom accelerators); concentrated buyer base with viable alternatives in development
- **Threat of Substitutes — Medium**: Custom accelerators (Broadcom, hyperscaler in-house silicon), CPUs for lighter inference workloads, and alternative software stacks can substitute in portions of the workload mix; full-stack GPU replacement remains difficult but partial displacement is credible
- **Competitive Rivalry — High**: Nvidia explicitly describes the market as intensely competitive; named rivals include AMD (Instinct GPUs, ROCm), Intel (Gaudi), Huawei (in export-restricted markets), and Broadcom (custom AI ASICs, AI networking); hyperscaler in-house chips represent a structural long-term competitive threat

### Supply Outlook

- Supply is currently **tight but expanding**: TSMC raised 2026 capex to the high end of $52B–$56B, stepping up N3 capacity for AI demand
- SK Hynix reports AI memory demand exceeds capacity; adding advanced-packaging capacity
- Micron describes a "strong demand environment" with "tight industry supply"
- New TSMC 3nm fab in Tainan targeted for volume production in H1 2027; Arizona second fab (3nm) in H2 2027; second Japan fab (3nm) in 2028
- Nvidia's Rubin platform production shipments expected in H2 FY2027
- Supply easing is incremental, not a step-change normalization; leading-edge capacity remains pulled by AI/HPC demand

### Demand Outlook

- Demand remains **strong and hyperscaler-anchored**:
  - Alphabet guided $175B–$185B 2026 capex, mostly technical infrastructure
  - Microsoft quarterly capex reached $34.9B driven by cloud and AI
  - Amazon 2025 cash capex was $128.3B, expected to increase in 2026; substantial AWS 2026 capex already has customer commitments
  - Meta spent $72.2B capex in 2025 explicitly tied to AI infrastructure
- OpenAI letter of intent (September 2025) targets first gigawatt of Vera Rubin systems for H2 2026
- WSTS projects global semiconductor market at $975B in 2026
- Dell'Oro forecasts 25% CAGR for data-center accelerator market over five years
- Gartner forecasts $2.52T in worldwide AI spending in 2026
- Key demand risk: AI-capex slowdown if hyperscaler ROI/monetization disappoints; datacenter power and grid constraints could delay deployments
