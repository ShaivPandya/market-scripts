# Analytical Playbook

This document defines the decision rubric for translating weekly macro, market, and positioning observations into analytical conclusions and stance adjustments. Every rule below is derived from the investment philosophy. No signal should be invented, and no threshold should be applied unless it is grounded in the framework.

---

## Stance Categories

Each weekly analysis must conclude with one of five stance designations. A stance is not a trade recommendation — it is a characterization of how the portfolio's overall posture should be oriented given the current environment.

### Aggressively Offensive

The environment presents rare, high-conviction opportunity. Multiple dimensions align: leading indicators are inflecting favorably, the market is confirming through sector leadership and breadth, liquidity is expanding or about to expand, and the consensus has not yet caught up. This is the fat pitch. Concentration increases. Position sizes move toward maximum. The analyst has earned the right to be aggressive through prior performance or the opportunity set is so exceptional that it overrides caution.

This stance is appropriate when: the cycle is turning from bust toward recovery, credit markets are reopening after having slammed shut, assets are priced at levels where perception is considerably worse than reality, or a secular regime shift is underway and the market is still in early innings.

### Offensive

Conditions favor risk-taking. The trend is intact, fundamentals are supportive, and the market is handling news constructively. Capital should be deployed into identified opportunities. Positions can be full-sized where conviction is high. The environment does not demand maximum aggression but rewards participation.

This stance is appropriate when: leading indicators are stable or improving, the market is trending with breadth confirmation, credit conditions are accommodative but not yet reckless, and the consensus view is broadly correct but the market still has room to move.

### Neutral / Watchful

No clear directional edge exists. Signals across dimensions are mixed, conflicting, or insufficient to support conviction. This is not a passive state — it is active vigilance. Capital preservation is the priority, but small probing positions are appropriate to maintain market contact, test hypotheses, and detect emerging signals. The bat stays on the shoulder until a fat pitch arrives.

This stance is appropriate when: leading indicators are ambiguous, the market is range-bound with no clear sector leadership, macro data is sending conflicting signals, or the analyst's recent performance has been poor and rhythm has not been re-established.

When there is nothing particularly clever to do, the potential pitfall lies in insisting on being clever.

### Defensive

Risk indicators are elevated. One or more of the following conditions are present: investor psychology has become euphoric, credit is being extended carelessly to marginal borrowers, valuations have stretched well beyond historical norms, leading real-economy indicators are deteriorating, or the market is failing to respond to good news. Reduce gross exposure. Tighten position sizes. Emphasize liquidity in all holdings. Shift toward assets and structures that perform in adverse scenarios.

This stance is appropriate when: the market is in the late innings of an expansion, the credit cycle is near its apex, risk aversion has evaporated, or multiple leading indicators are flashing amber simultaneously.

### Aggressively Defensive

Crisis conditions are forming, imminent, or underway. The priority is survival. Position for forced liquidation by others. Maintain maximum liquidity. Prepare a buy list for when the cycle turns — the best deployments happen at or near the bottom. You do not have to invest at the exact bottom, but you must be insulated from the forces that require selling and positioned to be a buyer when others cannot be.

This stance is appropriate when: a boom-bust sequence is entering its reversal phase, credit markets are seizing, forced selling is cascading through correlated positions, or a financial crisis is percolating. Historically, financial crises percolate for years but blow up in weeks.

## Key Analytical Dimensions

Evaluate conditions across six dimensions each week. No single dimension is decisive in isolation — the balance of evidence across all six determines the stance.

### 1. Market Behavior

What the market is doing matters more than what commentators say about it. The inside of the stock market is the best economist. Evaluate:

- **Sector internals**: Are leading cyclical groups (housing, trucking, retail, semiconductors, banks) outperforming or underperforming defensive groups (utilities, staples, pharmaceuticals)? When defensives lead and cyclicals weaken, the market is pricing in future economic deterioration regardless of what headline indices show.
- **Price response to news**: Bull markets ignore bad news and rally on good news. When good news stops producing rallies, or bad news starts mattering, the character of the market has changed.
- **Breadth**: When 75%+ of S&P 500 stocks are above their 200-day moving average, forward 12-month returns have historically averaged 11% with 85% positive hit rate. Extremely low breadth readings are often a sign of a market bottom. Narrowing breadth during an advance is a warning.
- **Volume patterns**: Sudden volume spikes in bombed-out securities signal accumulation. Low-volume rallies lack conviction. Volume confirms or denies the validity of price moves.
- **Technical structure**: 200-day moving averages, Fibonacci retracements, and rate-of-change indicators serve as confirmation tools. A long sideways consolidation followed by a sharp upmove on large volume is a high-signal pattern. When a stock or market sells off to the 200-day moving average coinciding with a 50% retracement, pay close attention.
- **Cross-market confirmation**: The strongest markets during a crisis tend to lead when pressure lifts. Markets dragged up by a related market that then rolls over may be good shorts. When intermarket relationships that have held for years begin to break down, something systemic may be changing.

### 2. Macro Data Momentum

Macro statistics tell you where you are and where you have been. They are poor predictors of where you are going. The signal comes from the rate of change and from real-economy leading sources.

- **Leading real-economy inputs**: What are management teams in housing, trucking, retail, and banking reporting about current trends? Conference call transcripts going back five years create a historical record of competence and truthfulness. One level below top management often provides the most honest signal.
- **Yield curve**: The 10-year minus 3-month Treasury spread is the best recession predictor. Persistent inversions (3+ months) are strong signals. The signal typically leads by about a year. The 10-year Treasury yield should trade approximately around nominal GDP — divergence from this relationship is informative.
- **Corporate profits trajectory**: Recessions have never begun before corporate profits peaked. If profits are still rising, a recession is unlikely to be imminent. If profits are rolling over, the clock is ticking.
- **Commodity signals**: Commodities are leading indicators for the economy. A 15%+ decline in a broad commodity index within 40 days during a tightening cycle is historically abnormal and suggests demand destruction. Always distinguish supply-driven from demand-driven moves — demand-driven declines are far more ominous.

### 3. Liquidity and Financial Conditions

Liquidity is what moves markets. Earnings do not move the overall market — the Federal Reserve does.

- **Central bank posture**: Is the Fed tightening, on hold, or easing? Rate-cutting modes historically create opportunity in the front end of the curve and in risk assets. The end of a hiking cycle has generally been a great time to buy stocks. But central banks manage by looking in the rearview mirror — they use lagging data — so their policy will often be inappropriate for current conditions.
- **Credit availability**: Is capital plentiful or scarce? Are terms easy or restrictive? Are lenders eager or reticent? When capital is in oversupply, investors compete for deals by accepting low returns and thin margins for error. When credit slams shut, bargains are created.
- **M2 relative to nominal GDP**: This ratio measures the degree to which the system has borrowed from its future. A 25% increase in M2 relative to nominal GDP represents a massive liquidity injection. Flat or declining ratios suggest no liquidity tailwind.
- **Spread behavior**: Credit spreads tell you what investors think the default rate will be, not what it actually will be. Narrow spreads indicate complacency. But narrow spreads alone are not sufficient reason to be bearish — the question is whether today's spread is sufficient to offset the credit losses that will actually occur. Spreads below historical averages may still be adequate if fundamental credit quality is strong.

### 4. Positioning

- **Speculative positioning**: Consistent net long positions building in futures among speculators after a period of being net short is a warning signal of crowded longs. The inverse — speculative shorts building after a period of long positioning — may signal a washout.
- **Option market signals**: Heavy buying of protective options means holders have underlying exposure they are nervous about. When everyone is buying insurance, a gestalt shift may be approaching — the big macro trades happen when the market is about to reprice a view entirely. Large imbalances in option positions, especially knock-out options, can trigger cascading moves.
- **Crowding assessment**: A trade being crowded does not automatically make it wrong — the crowd is right 80% of the time. But crowding affects entry points and increases the violence of any reversal. When a thesis is right and the trend confirms it, crowding is tolerable. When a thesis requires the crowd to be wrong, crowding is dangerous.
- **Flow analysis**: In currencies, a current account deficit currency that is rising implies a long position is building. A current account surplus currency that is falling implies a short is building. These buildups create the fuel for eventual reversals.

### 5. Risk Sentiment

- **Investor psychology spectrum**: Assess where investors currently sit on the spectrum from panic to euphoria. At the euphoric end: novel investment schemes are readily accepted, securities offerings are treated as opportunities to get rich, credit is freely available, and price-to-earnings ratios are elevated. At the panic end: investments are subjected to unreasonable scrutiny and endlessly negative assumptions, and people spend 100% of their time ensuring there can be no losses.
- **Risk tolerance indicators**: When incremental risk-taking is being rewarded and the lowest-quality assets are rallying hardest, risk tolerance is excessive. All the crap rallies at the end of a bull market — that is how you know it is near the end. Conversely, when quality outperforms junk decisively, risk aversion is returning.
- **Anecdotal signals**: When people you know who are not particularly sophisticated start getting rich, the market is likely peaking. When your friends who are not very smart are making a lot of money, there is too much credit around. Credit is about to contract.
- **Media and narrative**: When something is on the front page of the New York Times, regardless of how much you agree with the long-term thesis, the short-term consensus has already been formed. A contrarian pause is warranted even if the long-term view is unchanged.

### 6. Cycle Position

- **Where are we in the economic cycle?** Use the yield curve, leading indicators, and credit conditions to triangulate. Early cycle (recovery) favors aggressive offense. Mid cycle (expansion) favors selective offense. Late cycle (euphoria, loosening credit standards, rising leverage) favors defense. End of cycle (recession) favors aggressive defense transitioning to offense as the bottom forms.
- **Political cycle overlay**: Historically, buying the market two years before a general election and selling on the election has produced reliable bottoms in midterm election years. The third quarter of the final presidential year is typically below-average for stocks. The fourth quarter has 83% positive return frequency as election uncertainty resolves.
- **Secular vs. cyclical assessment**: Is the current environment a cyclical fluctuation within a stable long-term trend, or is a secular regime change underway? Secular shifts — in technology, monetary policy, geopolitics, demographics — create trends lasting years, not months. If this is a secular move, 10-month moves are just the beginning; even the dot-com bubble lasted 2-2.5 years, and for the core of the internet it lasted four.

## Interpreting Market Moves

### Confirmation Signals

The trend is intact and the thesis is being validated:

- The market rallies on good news and shrugs off bad news.
- Leading cyclical sectors are outperforming defensive sectors.
- Breadth is expanding — more stocks are participating in the advance.
- Volume is rising on advances, declining on pullbacks.
- Cross-market confirmation: related asset classes are moving in the expected direction.
- Rate-of-change indicators are accelerating in the direction of the thesis.
- Credit spreads are stable or narrowing.
- Currencies of fundamentally strong countries are appreciating.

### Warning Signals

Something is changing. Do not panic, but elevate monitoring and re-examine the thesis:

- Good news produces flat or negative price action in the market or in a specific position.
- Breadth is narrowing — fewer stocks are making new highs even as the index advances.
- Defensive sectors begin outperforming cyclicals without an obvious catalyst.
- Volume declines on rallies, expands on selloffs.
- Intermarket relationships that have held for an extended period begin to break down.
- Your P&L is behaving inconsistently with what you would expect from the current exposure matrix. This is the earliest warning — if things went well today and you are losing money, something is changing.
- A market that was dragged up by a related market begins to roll over.
- RSI reaching extreme oversold conditions in an individual stock — this is usually an acute phenomenon lasting only a few weeks and may present opportunity if whatever caused the selloff is already in the price.

### Regime Change Signals

A fundamental shift in market character is underway. Re-evaluate all existing positions and the overall stance:

- A break in an upward-sloping parabolic curve historically precedes downside acceleration.
- Speculators who had been consistently short switch to heavily net long (or vice versa).
- A currency move of extreme intensity lasting 11+ months — historically unprecedented and suggesting a secular shift.
- Credit markets slamming shut after an extended period of easy availability.
- Forced liquidation cascading through markets — the boom-bust reversal point has been reached.
- All the lowest-quality assets rally sharply — this is the terminal phase of a bull market.
- The gap between thinking and reality has become too wide, or participant bias has become too pronounced — the reflexive process is becoming self-defeating.
- A paradigm shift: everyone is still focusing on what used to matter while the world has changed.

## Interpreting Macro Data

### Principle

Do not trade macro statistics directly. They are lagging. Use them only to confirm or deny what leading real-economy indicators and market internals are already suggesting.

### Employment Data

Employment is a lagging indicator. Low unemployment does not mean the economy is safe — most recessions were preceded by very low unemployment. The economy runs out of capacity. Use employment reports primarily as tactical tools: they move the Fed and thus create entry and exit points to fade.

### GDP and Output Data

GDP tells you where you have been. By the time more than 50% of economists in the Philly Fed survey project a decline in real GDP in the coming quarter, the recession is over or close to being over. Use this as a bottom indicator, not a predictive tool.

### Inflation Data

Distinguish between the signal and the policy response. Inflation above 4.5% has never been resolved with a soft landing. Every deflation has followed an asset bubble — deflation is not caused by low inflation but by the unwinding of excess. If inflation is running hot, assess whether the policy response will be sufficient and whether asset prices have adjusted.

### Interest Rate Decisions

The end of a hiking cycle is historically a good time to buy risk assets. But watch for the gap between the Fed's backward-looking assessment and the forward-looking reality. If the front end of the curve is already pricing rate cuts, staying long the front end remains appropriate if you believe a recession is truly coming.

### Credit Data

Credit conditions lead the economy. Deteriorating credit card quality, rising delinquencies, and tightening lending standards signal economic weakness ahead — even if headline data looks fine. Conversely, easing credit conditions after a crunch signal the early stages of recovery.

## Positioning Signals

### Crowded Longs

When a trade is crowded long, the thesis may still be correct, but:

- Entry points become poor — expect limited upside from here unless a new catalyst emerges.
- Reversal risk is elevated — any disappointment can trigger a stampede.
- The best thing that can happen is often a washout that allows re-entry at better prices.
- Do not avoid a trade solely because it is crowded if the thesis is right and the trend confirms. But adjust position size for the entry risk.

### Crowded Shorts

When a market or security is heavily shorted:

- Short interest alone is not a buy signal, but it creates the fuel for violent rallies.
- If nobody owns something, demand (and thus the price) can only go up.
- Whatever asset is considered the worst at a given point in time has a good likelihood of being the cheapest.
- Going from taboo to merely tolerated can produce substantial returns.

### Extreme Positioning Signals

- Speculators flipping from consistent net short to heavy net long in futures (as observed before the 1987 crash) signals that the character of market participation has changed.
- Heavy protective option buying across the market means a potential gestalt shift is approaching.
- When volatility has been suppressed for an extended period, position buildups accumulate beneath the surface. Lack of volatility leads to volatility.

## Risk Signals

### Escalating Risk Environment

The following conditions, especially in combination, indicate that the risk environment is deteriorating:

1. **Credit cycle apex indicators**: Capital is plentiful, terms are easy, lenders are eager, spreads are narrow, and borrowers of declining quality are accessing capital markets.
2. **Leverage elevation**: Borrowing levels are high relative to historical averages. Intraday moves are large relative to weekly net moves, indicating leveraged positioning in the system.
3. **Complacency indicators**: Risk aversion has evaporated. Novel investment schemes are being accepted without scrutiny. Everyone claims to be outperforming. Unsophisticated participants are making money.
4. **Correlation breakdown**: Assets that should be uncorrelated are moving together. Previously reliable intermarket relationships are diverging. The P&L is behaving unexpectedly — this is the earliest warning that correlations are shifting.
5. **Reflexive self-reinforcement approaching exhaustion**: A trend that has been self-reinforcing is reaching the point where the gap between perception and reality is too wide, or participant bias is too extreme. The twilight period of a boom-bust sequence.
6. **Policy error risk**: The central bank is tightening into a weakening economy (managing by rearview mirror) or has maintained excessively loose policy long enough to inflate an asset bubble. Governments driving 90 miles an hour at a brick wall have not yet hit the brakes.
7. **Liquidity illusion**: Low realized volatility is being mistaken for low risk. Positions that were liquid on the way in may not be liquid on the way out. Relative value trades with negative gamma are particularly dangerous — they produce smooth returns until they produce catastrophic ones.

### Declining Risk Environment (Opportunity Emerging)

1. Credit markets have slammed shut and are beginning to reopen.
2. Forced selling is exhausting itself.
3. Leading cyclical indicators are stabilizing after a decline.
4. Extreme pessimism is universal — when everyone agrees there is no merit in something.
5. Asset prices have declined to levels where the fundamentals alone justify the price without requiring any optimistic assumptions.
6. The political cycle is in the midterm trough window (two years before a general election).

## Conflict Resolution

When analytical dimensions send contradictory signals, apply the following hierarchy:

### Rule 1: Market Price Action Is the Tiebreaker

If fundamentals say one thing and the market says another, respect the market. The market may know something you do not. This does not mean abandon the thesis — it means elevate scrutiny and reduce position size until the discrepancy resolves.

### Rule 2: Leading Indicators Override Lagging Indicators

If employment data and GDP say the economy is strong but housing is weakening, trucking is weakening, and credit card delinquencies are rising, weight the leading indicators more heavily. The headline data will eventually catch up.

### Rule 3: The P&L Is the Honest Signal

If you cannot explain why your portfolio is behaving the way it is, something you do not understand is happening. Investigate before adding risk. When the P&L starts acting strange — especially when it should be performing well based on the day's events but is not — antennae should go up immediately.

### Rule 4: When Confused, Do Nothing

Do not take positions without well-founded convictions. You are liable to be faked out by stray fluctuations when you lack a clear thesis. Random walks lead to getting mugged. Inaction is a legitimate and often optimal analytical conclusion.

### Rule 5: Consider the Asymmetry

When signals conflict, ask: what happens if I am wrong in each direction? If being wrong on the bullish side means limited downside but being wrong on the bearish side means catastrophic loss, the conflict should resolve toward defense. If being wrong on the bearish side means missing a moderate gain but being wrong on the bullish side means a small, manageable loss, the conflict can resolve toward offense.

### Rule 6: Time Horizon Reconciliation

Signals can conflict because they operate on different timeframes. A short-term overbought market can exist within a long-term bullish trend. Distinguish between signals that affect the next few weeks versus those that affect the next 18 months. Adjust positioning and sizing accordingly — the long-term view determines direction; the short-term signals determine timing and entry.

## Escalation Triggers

The following developments warrant immediate re-evaluation of the current stance, even mid-week:

### Trigger Immediate Review

- The yield curve inverts (10-year minus 3-month goes negative) or steepens violently after a sustained inversion.
- A broad commodity index drops 10%+ in less than 30 days.
- Credit spreads widen 100+ basis points in a week without an identifiable transient cause.
- Speculative positioning flips directionally (e.g., net short to net long or vice versa) in a major market.
- The P&L moves more than 50 basis points in a direction inconsistent with what market events suggest it should.
- A major sovereign or financial institution shows signs of liquidity stress (drawing down credit lines, asset fire sales).
- Market breadth collapses below 30% of S&P 500 stocks above the 200-day moving average.
- A major currency experiences a multi-standard-deviation move in a single session.

### Trigger Closer Monitoring

- Good news is met with flat or negative market reaction for two or more consecutive sessions.
- Defensive sectors begin outperforming cyclicals for a sustained period (3+ weeks) without a clear catalyst.
- Credit card delinquencies or bank charge-offs begin trending higher.
- Conference call commentary from companies in leading industries shifts from optimistic to cautious.
- Volume patterns shift — rallies occur on declining volume, selloffs on expanding volume.
- Options skew shifts meaningfully — demand for downside protection increases sharply.
- An industry or asset class transitions from universally adored to controversial.
- A parabolic price curve breaks its upward-sloping trendline.

## Weekly Analysis Process

Follow this sequence each week. Each step builds on the prior step. Do not skip steps or reorder them.

### Step 1: Identify Major Changes Since Last Week

Begin by cataloguing what has changed. Not what happened — what changed. A data release that confirms the existing trend is not a change. A data release that contradicts the existing trend, or a market that responds to familiar news in an unfamiliar way, is a change.

Specifically document:
- Any market moves that are inconsistent with the prevailing thesis.
- Any leading indicators that have shifted direction.
- Any credit or liquidity conditions that have tightened or loosened.
- Any positioning data that shows meaningful shifts.
- Any management commentary from leading industries that contradicts recent trends.
- Any policy actions or statements that alter the macro landscape.

### Step 2: Evaluate Signals Across All Six Dimensions

For each of the six analytical dimensions (Market Behavior, Macro Data Momentum, Liquidity and Financial Conditions, Positioning, Risk Sentiment, Cycle Position), assign one of four assessments:

- **Supportive**: The dimension favors continued or increased risk-taking.
- **Neutral**: The dimension provides no clear directional signal.
- **Cautionary**: The dimension suggests rising risk or deteriorating conditions.
- **Adverse**: The dimension actively signals danger or the need to reduce exposure.

Provide specific evidence for each assessment. No assessment should be made without citing at least one concrete observation from the week's data.

### Step 3: Determine the Overall Balance of Evidence

Weigh the six dimensional assessments. Not all dimensions carry equal weight at all times:

- In normal environments, Market Behavior and Liquidity/Financial Conditions are the heaviest weights.
- When the cycle is at an extreme (peak euphoria or peak panic), Risk Sentiment and Cycle Position become the heaviest weights.
- When macro data is inflecting (turning from improvement to deterioration or vice versa), Macro Data Momentum temporarily rises in priority.
- When a market has moved sharply and positioning is extreme, the Positioning dimension rises in priority.

State the overall balance explicitly: how many dimensions are supportive, neutral, cautionary, or adverse? Is the weight concentrated in the most important dimensions for the current environment?

### Step 4: Assign Stance

Based on the balance of evidence, assign one of the five stance categories. If the stance has changed from the prior week, state:

- What changed to warrant the shift.
- Whether the shift is tentative (requiring confirmation next week) or firm (supported by multiple converging signals).
- What would cause a reversal of the new stance.

If the stance has not changed, state what would need to change to move it in either direction.

### Step 5: Define Watchlist Triggers

Identify 3-5 specific, observable events or data points that would trigger a stance reassessment before the next weekly review. These should be concrete and falsifiable — not vague concerns but specific thresholds or occurrences.

For each trigger, specify:
- What the trigger is (e.g., "S&P 500 breadth falls below 50% of stocks above 200-DMA").
- Which direction it would push the stance (more offensive or more defensive).
- What action it would imply (reduce exposure, add to a position, initiate a new position, exit an existing position).

### Step 6: Identify the Trade After the Trade

Ask: if the current thesis plays out as expected, what happens next? What is the second-order consequence? The best analysts do not just identify the current opportunity — they anticipate what the market will focus on once the current consensus view becomes priced in.

This step prevents complacency after a correct call. The market is always moving forward. After making money on a correct thesis, the question is immediately: now what?
